# R
**Inspiring an entirely new field of drug discovery focused on targeted protein degradation and molecular glues**

---

## Molecular Glue

A molecular glue is a small molecule that causes two proteins to interact when they normally would not, or stabilizes a weak interaction between them.

In targeted protein degradation, the glue often forces a target protein to bind an E3 ligase complex.

---

## DCAF Proteins

DCAF stands for DDB1- and CUL4-Associated Factor. They are substrate receptors in the CRL4 E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, which is one of the cell’s protein disposal systems.

Their job is to recognize specific proteins and present them to the ubiquitination machinery so those proteins can be tagged for destruction by the proteasome.

DDB1 and CUL4 are like the “machine,” while the DCAF determines which protein gets selected for disposal. Different DCAFs recognize different targets.

Once the target is brought close enough to the ubiquitination machinery, it gets tagged with ubiquitin and destroyed.

---

## RING Finger Proteins

A RING finger protein is a protein that contains a specialized structural domain called a RING (Really Interesting New Gene) finger domain. This domain coordinates zinc ions and allows the protein to mediate protein-protein interactions. Many function as E3 ubiquitin ligases.

---

## Drug Discovery

For decades, many disease proteins were considered “undruggable” because they lacked enzyme active sites or pockets for conventional drugs.

DCAF-based molecular glues offer a different strategy: instead of inhibiting the protein, recruit it to a cellular shredder.

This potentially allows degradation of transcription factors, scaffolding proteins, and other targets that traditional small molecules struggle to affect.
